= DSI: Data model (Firestore)
:toc:
:lang: en

The zone assignment is *not stored*: it is computed with `member = members[(week + zoneIndex) % memberCount]`. Only data that cannot be derived is stored.

[source]
----
flats/{flatId}
  name, startDate, zones[], tricountUrl (optional, https on tricount.com only), inviteCode, createdAt
flats/{flatId}/members/m{order}
  name, color, uid (anonymous identity that claimed it, or null), active, order (position in the rotation)
flats/{flatId}/users/{uid}
  memberId (membership: links an anonymous identity to its member)
flats/{flatId}/completions/{weekIndex}_{zone}
  weekIndex, zone, memberId, doneAt (YYYY-MM-DD)
inviteCodes/{code}
  flatId, ownerUid, flatName (so a joiner sees the flat name before joining)
userFlats/{uid}/flats/{flatId}
  memberId, flatName (the flats of an identity: how a Google account finds its flats from a new phone)
owners/{uid}
  flatCount (flats created by this identity, at most 3)
----

== Modeling decisions
* The completion identifier combines week and zone: marking again corrects the date without duplicating.
* `order` keeps the order in which the members were entered: the rotation depends on it and Firestore does not guarantee any order by identifier.
* `active` allows deactivating a member without breaking the history.
* `uid` links a member to the anonymous identity of their device.
* Member ids are `m0` to `m19` and must match `order`: this is how the rules cap a flat at 20 people, because a collection cannot be counted in a rule.
* `owners/{uid}` counts the flats an identity created. A flat can only be created in a batch that raises the counter by exactly one, and the counter cannot exceed 3.

== Access rules (NFR-02)
Implemented in `firebase/firestore/firestore.rules` and verified with tests on the emulator (`npm run test:rules`).

. A user is a *member* of a flat if `flats/{flatId}/users/{uid}` exists. Only members read the flat and its completions.
. Create a flat: only a user whose identity is linked to Google (`request.auth.token.firebase.identities` contains `google.com`), who becomes the owner (`ownerUid`, immutable). A flat can never be deleted, nor its members (they are deactivated with `active`).
. Join: the user claims an unclaimed member (`uid == null`) and creates their membership in the same batch. An already claimed member cannot be claimed.
. Only the owner edits the flat and adds or modifies members.
. Completions: a member only records, corrects or deletes their own, for an existing zone, with a valid date and a `week_zone` identifier.
. Invite codes: they can be read, not listed. Creating one requires the `flatName` to match the flat.
. `userFlats/{uid}`: each identity reads and writes only its own list. Flatmates join anonymously and may link Google later (the uid is kept), so the same list works for them.
. Abuse limits: at most 3 flats per identity, at most 20 people per flat and completion weeks up to 1040 (about 20 years).

=== Limits and their reach
With anonymous sign-in, an identity is in practice a browser profile on a device. The limits bound what each identity and each flat can hold and prevent accidents, but they do not stop a determined attacker, because a new anonymous identity is free. The real protection against abuse is Firebase App Check and quota alerts (issue https://github.com/Glinbor10/housely/issues/20[#20]).

=== Conscious trade-off
Member names (`members`) are readable by any authenticated user, so that whoever joins can pick theirs before becoming a member. The flat identifier is random and is only obtained through the invite code. The rest of the flat's data is not exposed.
